How it works

You upload the documents you already teach with. The tool reads them, checks each one against a set of teaching-quality rules, and reports what is missing and how to fix it. Nothing you upload is changed.

  1. Material

    1. Upload your material

    Add your PDFs and tick what each one is: syllabus, slides, assessment, or rubric. A file can be more than one type, for example an exam that includes its own rubric.

    The type decides which rules are checked on it.

  2. Rules

    2. Choose your rules

    A rule is one thing a document must do. Your course starts with a ready-made set based on good teaching practice. Turn any off, or write your own in plain language and the tool builds the check for you.

    You decide what "good" means for your course.

  3. Analysis

    3. Run the analysis

    Every rule is checked against every document it applies to and marked pass or fail. The health score is the share of checks that passed, counting a high-severity rule more than a minor one.

    One number for the course, plus the detail behind it.

  4. Report

    4. Read the report and improve

    Each failed check names the document, the rule, and the passage that caused it, with a concrete fix. Edit the file, upload the new version, and re-run to watch the score rise. The report exports to PDF.

    The goal is to close the gaps, not just find them.

Writing a good rule

Write it as a requirement and be specific, so it can be checked.

  • "Each assessment must state which learning outcomes it evaluates."
  • "At least 60% of exam questions must require reasoning, not recall."
  • "The material should be engaging." (too vague to check)

Nothing is added until you review the draft and click Accept.

Two assistants

There is a chat on the Analysis page and another on Material. They do different jobs.

  • In the report, it explains why something was flagged and rewrites the suggestions with you: ask for "make this shorter" or push back on advice you disagree with, and the block updates in place. Type @ to aim it at one document.
  • In your material, it answers questions about what your documents actually say, quoting the page it took each answer from.

Neither one edits your uploaded files, and the pass/fail findings stay as the analysis produced them, only the written suggestions can be reworded.

Ready to try it

A syllabus and one set of slides is enough for a first run. You can add the rest later, and re-run as often as you like.
